# Scoring hooks

Score every hook before you show it to the user. The rules come from the user's own notes on drafts (the four anchors at the bottom) and from what tends to hold the first three seconds. A score is a judgement, not a forecast: the ledger's "viewed vs swiped away" is the real test.

A hook has four parts. Write all four before scoring; a hook without its turn can't be scored.

- **Say**: the spoken line.
- **Text**: the words on screen at frame 0.
- **Frame 0**: what the first frame shows, as if the video were paused mid-action.
- **Turn**: the line or shot later in the short that pays the hook off.

## Gates

If a hook fails one, rewrite it before scoring.

1. **Lands in under three seconds** when read aloud. Time it.
2. **The turn delivers exactly what the hook promised.** A hook the short can't pay off is bait, and the swipe rate punishes it.
3. **True for whoever says it.** An "I" hook must have happened to the person on camera. If it didn't, use a peer ("my manager…") or the forward shape ("here's what I'd do…").
4. **About slides, the room or the deck.** A provocation off the topic wins views and no users: Cluely got 49M views on one video and fewer than 100 downloads.
5. **Viewer first, never Occam first.** No brand in the first words, no ask ("follow", "sign up", "link in bio"), no welcome or intro.

## Score

Give each line 0, 1 or 2. Lines 1 to 4 come from the user's notes and weigh the most: a 0 on any of them means rewrite, whatever the total.

| # | Question | 0 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Plain words?** Anyone, not just office workers, gets it on mute in one read. | Insider shorthand: "so what", MECE, issue tree, hypothesis, synthesis, SCQA, storyline, pyramid, red pen, partner, "deck", "one line". | Words a general viewer uses about a scene they can picture: "slides", "my boss sent it back", "say the answer first". |
| 2 | **Is the outcome envied by an outsider?** Judge by what other people do, not by what the deck is. | Insider metrics nobody outside consulting rates: "41 slides to 6", "tighter storyline". | Someone with power reacts: the boss signs off, the CEO says yes, the investor takes the meeting, the deck goes up the chain unedited. |
| 3 | **Dialled up?** Most result for least effort, at the edge of believable. | Modest or slow: "in one evening", "eventually", "better". | Big result, tiny effort: "one prompt", "first read", "slide three". Must still pass gate 2. |
| 4 | **Holds the answer?** The hook names what kind of fix it is and keeps the fix for the turn. Text and Frame 0 hold it too. | The fix is in the hook: "I'd write only the titles first"; or Frame 0 shows it (a deck of titles only). | A shaped gap: "here's what I'd do to get my proposal approved"; "one habit changed". The viewer knows what kind of answer is coming, not which. |
| 5 | **Specific?** | Vague tease: "this changed everything", "you won't believe". | A concrete noun, person or number: "slide three", "the CEO", "unedited". |
| 6 | **Three layers agree?** | Text repeats the say word for word, or Frame 0 is a static face or an empty slide. | Text in 3 to 7 words sharpens the say ("Yes. Slide three."); Frame 0 shows the stake or the result mid-action and works muted. |
| 7 | **Does the viewer see themselves?** | The character has an advantage the viewer lacks: a design team, a famous client, a paid course. | The maker, a peer, or "you", with nothing the viewer can't have. |
| 8 | **On Occam's ground?** | Sells speed alone ("in seconds"), where Gamma already wins. | Thinking and explaining clearly: being understood, the yes, looking like you know your stuff. Speed only as the means. |

**Ship at 12 or more out of 16 with no 0 on lines 1 to 4.** Report the score as `13/16 (clear 2, envy 2, dial 1, holds 2, …)`, then one line on the weakest part and how you'd raise it.

## Funnel stage

Label every hook with the job it does. Each stage has its own shape and its own number in the ledger.

| Stage | Job | Hook shape | Judged by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Top: eyeballs** | Stop people who didn't know they had the problem. | Recognition or a contrarian line about thinking or explaining. No Occam in the hook. | Viewed vs swiped away |
| **Middle: followers** | Give a reason to come back. | A reusable method, named as part of a series ("Think clearly, part 2"). | Follows, saves and shares per view |
| **Bottom: buyers** | Turn someone who wants the result into a user. | A desire hook: a relatable person gets the outcome, and Occam does it on screen. | Taps on the short's own path, then sign-ups |

Every turn shows the fix done in Occam, at every stage; the tip must still work for someone who never opens it. For a top hook, line 2 reads as "is the damage something anyone fears?"

## In a batch

- Three hooks, three different kinds (problem, result, contrarian, error, number, quote, desire), not one idea reworded. The ledger's hook style column names the kind.
- Label each hook top, middle or bottom. A batch for one short usually has a top and a bottom option.
- Recommend the highest scorer, unless the ledger says its kind lost last time.

## Anchors

These are the user's notes on real drafts. Score new hooks against them.

| Draft | Note | Lines failed | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|---|
| I stopped getting "so what?" by rewriting one line on every slide. | "not very clear" | 1 clear, 4 holds | My boss stopped sending my decks back. One habit changed. |
| I went from 41 slides to 6 in one afternoon. One change. | "not everyone knows that is good, neither it is impressive" | 2 envy | The CEO said yes on slide three. |
| I rebuilt my investor deck in one evening. No designer. | "dial up, may be you rebuilt it in one prompt?" | 3 dial | I built my investor deck from one prompt. They took the meeting. |
| If I wanted my boss to approve first time, I'd write only the titles first. | "you kill the intrigue. you need to keep the answer till later" | 4 holds | Here's what I'd do to get my proposal approved first time. |

General note on the first batch: "I don't like this overly red pen / partner jargon. Use clear words, understood by the general public." That is line 1, and it covers turns and Frame 0 too.

When the user leaves a new note on a hook, add it here as an anchor. If it names something the rubric doesn't cover, add a line or a gate.
